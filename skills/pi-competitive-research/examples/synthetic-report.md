## Three proposed experiments

**1. Check whether a language-service sentence answers the visitor's question.** E01 and E02 suggest a gap between a supplied business fact and the reviewed text. After the owner confirms current staffing and hours, a marketer could draft a contact-page sentence and compare two mockups with six volunteer adults over one week. Ask each whether Spanish-language help is offered and when. Record correct answers out of six for each mockup and the reasons for confusion. Proposed decision: revise any statement participants misread; consider publishing only after the firm confirms its accuracy. This is a small comprehension exercise, not evidence of conversion lift. No participants have been recruited.

**2. Investigate after-hours wording.** Cedar's callback-window statement and Elm's different availability claim raise a communication question, not a service-quality conclusion. The office manager could spend one hour comparing Cedar's existing internal coverage schedule with its published contact instructions, then draft wording for two scenarios: office hours and after hours. Proposed decision: retain only claims the current workflow supports, and have the owner review any discrepancy. The metric is the number of unsupported or ambiguous statements found and resolved in the draft. No calls, account changes, or website edits are part of this research. [E01, E04]

**3. Collect a repeatable local search baseline.** Missing search evidence is the reason for this experiment. A marketer could document the same two queries—`personal injury lawyer Example City` and `truck accident lawyer Example City`—from one known observer location on two dates a week apart, using the same device and language. Record Paid Search, LSA, unpaid local/Maps, and organic results separately, plus any sponsored local placements. Proposed decision: use the results to choose one channel for deeper investigation only if the snapshots are comparable; collect missing conditions before drawing a trend. Four planned query observations do not establish demand or market share. This is a proposed collection plan; no query was run.
