# Evidence and comparison guide

Use this guide for every report. The aim is a comparison a busy firm owner can act on, with a clear path from the source to the suggestion.

## Scope and selection

Record the subject firm, website, target market, priority practice areas, research question, collection dates, and relevant capabilities or access limits. Keep user-supplied business facts separate from public claims: “The owner reports Spanish intake is staffed” and “The contact page advertises Spanish intake” are different evidence.

For each competitor, give its canonical website, observed local connection, practice overlap, and selection reason. Search discovery is a way to find candidates, not a market census. Verify similar names, office locations, redirects, and domains before attributing claims. If a site cannot be verified, label the identity unresolved and omit unsupported comparisons. Do not silently replace a user-named firm; explain any unusable entry. Research fewer than five if that is all the verified, relevant evidence supports.

## Website coverage

Start with these pages; add pages only when a material comparison needs them:

| Page | Capture | Limit of the observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Main promise, case types, geography, displayed contact options | A published promise is a marketing claim |
| Priority practice page(s) | Types of matters described, intended reader, visible next step | A page's existence does not prove capability, demand, or outcomes |
| Location/about page | Claimed service area and listed offices | Serving a city and maintaining an office there are distinct |
| Contact page | Phone/form/chat links, instructions, office hours, availability wording | Do not submit, call, chat, or test response time |
| Language page or selector, if present | Exact language named, translated content, interpreter or bilingual claim | Translated content does not establish staffed intake in that language |

Keep attorney-access promises, consultation offers, fee claims, testimonials, awards, and reported results in the firm's own terms with attribution. A listed award or result is not an independent quality rating. Avoid repeating promotional superlatives as the report's voice.

For contact usability, report what was visible and how it was inspected: “Phone link visible in the mobile header at 390 px” requires an actual rendered mobile view; text extraction supports only “Phone number present in extracted page text.” A form's visible fields can be described without entering anything. Do not infer successful submission, accessibility conformance, response speed, or ease for real users from markup alone. “Open 24 hours” on a profile and “office hours 9–5” on a site can concern different services; preserve the discrepancy.

Prefer paraphrase. Use brief attributed excerpts only when precise wording matters. Do not reproduce full pages, ads, or review collections. A search snippet can help discovery, but it is not equivalent to reading the linked page; if it is all that is accessible, label it as a snippet and limit the claim accordingly.

## Evidence ledger

Give each observation an ID, such as `E01`. A source may support more than one observation; distinguish them when their scope differs. Every material statement in the matrix, findings, or experiments must point to its evidence IDs, and the appendix must link to the sources.

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| ID / firm / channel | Stable evidence ID, verified entity, website/search channel/profile/review/export |
| Observation | A narrow description of what the source actually displayed; keep interpretation separate |
| Source | Exact inspected URL and page title; for supplied artifacts, filename/page/row plus original URL if provided |
| Observed or captured at | Access date; time and timezone for search/ads if available; distinguish artifact capture date from date received |
| Method | Rendered browser page, text extraction, search-result snippet, user screenshot, user export, etc. |
| Status / coverage | One of the statuses below and the inspected pages/viewport/range relevant to it |
| Query / limitations | Query ID for search, review sample ID for review text, and important uncertainty |

Use precise statuses:

- **Observed:** Source displayed the stated claim or feature. Add “firm claim,” “reviewer claim,” or “user-supplied fact” where applicable.
- **Not stated in reviewed pages:** Those pages were inspected and contained no explicit statement about that topic. Name the pages; this does not establish absence across the site or business.
- **Not observed in sample:** The relevant search channel or review sample was inspected, but the item was not present within the recorded scope. This does not establish absence generally.
- **Unavailable:** An attempted source or channel could not be inspected. Record why, such as blocked access or ads omitted by the tool.
- **Not checked:** Outside scope or deliberately skipped. State why if it matters.
- **Conflicting:** Sources disagree. Cite both and do not choose a convenient answer without further evidence.

“Not observed” is not valid when the tool cannot expose that channel. A blank cell, dash, zero, and “none” are too ambiguous for missing research.

## Public review sampling

Review themes are optional supporting evidence, not a measure of legal quality. Do not base a competitor's service rating on selected complaints or testimonials.

Use one common platform where possible and predeclare a repeatable rule. A reasonable small default is the five most recent accessible text-bearing reviews per firm under a newest-first sort. Record platform, profile URL, sort/filter, collection date, number accessible and number sampled, displayed total/rating if inspected, and the sample's publication-date range. If newest-first is unavailable, report the actual displayed order and call it a convenience sample. Do not relabel “most relevant” as recent. Disclose exclusions, such as rating-only or duplicate entries, and do not change the rule to collect more positive or negative reviews for a firm.

For each sampled text, record a review permalink when accessible, displayed publication date, brief paraphrase, and theme. If there is no permalink, cite the profile plus an artifact/page locator or visible excerpt sufficient to locate it without republishing a person's name or case details. If reproduction is restricted, keep the summary and access note only. Relative dates stay approximate; do not turn “a month ago” into an exact day.

Keep three concepts separate:

1. Displayed profile rating and review count at a stated time.
2. Themes in the particular texts sampled, such as “Two of five sampled texts mentioned status updates.”
3. An interpretation to test, such as making communication expectations clearer on the subject firm's site.

State that reviewers self-select, platforms may filter or order reviews, accessible texts may be incomplete, and the sample is small. Firm-selected testimonials have an additional selection bias. Treat reviewer statements as attributed accounts; do not infer representativeness, truth of an allegation, causation, fake reviews, client satisfaction across the firm, or differences in legal competence. Avoid comparative theme percentages when sample methods or periods differ. Skip the theme section if the actual text cannot be inspected.

## Findings and experiments

A useful finding has three parts: **observation with sources → interpretation → uncertainty**. For example, a contact page that does not mention a user-confirmed language service may justify testing clearer language information. It does not establish that competitors answer in that language, that the market wants the service, or that the change will increase signed cases.

For each of three to five proposed experiments, specify:

- The evidence and business question it addresses.
- A falsifiable hypothesis and one primary change or bounded research task.
- The owner role, prerequisite facts, timebox or sample cap, and approximate effort.
- A primary metric with denominator, baseline/comparison if available, and proposed continue/stop decision.
- What a positive or negative result would and would not establish.

Choose relevant options: a language-service message test, clearer contact instructions, a practice-page comprehension check, a repeatable local search sample, or a review-theme validation exercise. Do not automatically recommend ad spend or an intake tool. For a small firm with little traffic, a limited comprehension exercise may be more honest than a supposedly conclusive conversion test. If no valid baseline or sample plan exists, make establishing one the first step. Label invented example thresholds as proposals; never present them as industry benchmarks. All changes, participant recruitment, and account work remain proposed follow-up work.
